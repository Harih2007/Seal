01 · SUNLIGHT ZONE0 — 200 M

Clear light.
Busy water.

Sunlight reaches the upper ocean. Coastal schools and open-water fish move through changing glare, surface shimmer, and suspended particles.

IN THE MODEL LABEL SETMugil cephalusFlathead grey mullet · coastal exampleCoryphaena hippurusCommon dolphinfish · open-water example
DETECTION CHALLENGEGlare, waves, and crowded schools

Reflections and overlapping fish can split one school into noisy boxes or hide small individuals.

Response

Track detections across frames, tune confidence against empty-water footage, and review annotated clips.

Species shown are examples present in the Fishial classifier metadata, not confirmed local sightings.
02
02 · TWILIGHT ZONE200 — 1,000 M

Blue fades.
Contrast gets hard.

Sunlight falls away and color disappears. Some pelagic fish dive into this dim midwater and return upward later.

IN THE MODEL LABEL SETThunnus obesusBigeye tuna · known for deep divesXiphias gladiusSwordfish · dives through deeper water
DETECTION CHALLENGELow contrast and motion blur

Fish edges blend into blue water; longer exposure can smear fast movement.

Response

Use a stable light source, short exposure where possible, and larger inference size for small distant targets.

Depth use varies by species and life stage; these are broad examples, not fixed depth limits.
03
03 · MIDNIGHT ZONE1,000 — 4,000 M

No sunlight.
Only evidence.

Below the reach of sunlight, camera lighting becomes the scene. The current classifier label set has limited deep-sea representation, so this zone is a coverage gap to measure—not a promise of species ID.

MODEL COVERAGEDeep-sea labels are limitedDo not force a familiar species label onto an unfamiliar fish.Unknown fish → reviewKeep the detection track and flag its species as unlabelled.
DETECTION CHALLENGEDarkness, backscatter, and tiny targets

Lighting can illuminate particles in front of the lens, creating bright false detections.

Response

Angle or diffuse paired lights, control exposure, compare enhanced and original frames, then abstain when species evidence is weak.

Deep-sea fish silhouettes are illustrative; they do not claim to be classifier classes.
04
04 · ABYSS & TRENCHES4,000 M +

Pressure. Darkness.
Unlabelled life.

At abyssal depths there is no sunlight, and specialized pressure-rated cameras and lighting are essential. A classifier trained on other species cannot reliably name what it has never learned.

WHAT SEAL SHOULD REPORTFish detected · species unlabelledPreserve the box, temporary track ID, confidence, and image-pixel path for expert review.Coverage gap recordedBuild a verified deep-sea dataset before adding species claims.
DETECTION CHALLENGEExtreme darkness and pressure

Sensor noise, backscatter, and unfamiliar body shapes can confuse both detection and classification.

Response

Use pressure-rated imaging with controlled strobed light, keep detection separate from species ID, and route uncertain tracks for human review.

Depth bands are simplified oceanographic zones. Inference from video does not establish actual water depth.
